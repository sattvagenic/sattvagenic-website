    Inside Nadi

    An instrument on the left, the sound on the right. Right-click almost anything for more.

    Seven chakras

    Seven tuned resonators that ring with your sound, each on its own note of the raga, with its own level, pan, decay and Aux output: send one to a reverb, or take it out of the mix to treat it alone.

    The Shakti

    The energy that plays them. She climbs and falls through the chakras in time with your song, opening each one to the sound as she passes, so they ring in rhythm and melody. Slide the groove with Offset, and write four phrases that follow an ORDER you choose.

    28 materials

    Earthly: stone, ghatam, bells, a singing bowl, tuned skins. Subtle: volcanic glass, resin, crystal strings, light, singing water. BODY dials reshape each one, and Bend adds meend, andolan and gamak.

    Motion

    Two LFOs move any dial or fader. Right-click, pick how far it swings, and it breathes. An arc shows its reach and a bead where the sound is now.

    Ananda & Grace

    Turn up Ananda and a raga-true chord opens over the chakras. Switch on Grace and chords bloom by themselves now and then; lock one you love to keep it.

    Inspire & Mutate

    Click the yantra for a whole new sound in your source's style, or Mutate for a gentle variation. Locks keep the materials, body, motion, raga or melody you want to hold on to.

    • 85 presets in Melodic, Perc, Bass, Drone and Starter
    • 14 ragas in just intonation, plus Major, Minor and your own Custom tuning
    • Four Nadis: Ida, Pingala, Sushumna (with sympathetic taraf) and Balanced
    • MIDI In plays Sa from your keyboard, with glide
    • An internal mix engine shapes each chakra before the seven are summed
    • A japa counter: her rounds as beads on a 108-bead mala
    • 32-step undo, with Go back and Go forward
    • Free tuning files so your synths can play in Nadi's ragas
    • Native on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, and 64-bit Windows

    Chakra-Inspired Resonance

    Nadi grew out of the Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra, a Kashmiri text of 112 ways into stillness, several of them through sound: listening to a stringed instrument until only the listening remains, or following a chanted syllable as it fades into silence.

    The chakras ring with the source; the Shakti climbs between them; the three channels of yogic philosophy, Ida, Pingala and Sushumna, give each patch its character. It works just as well as a quiet companion for practice: chant with it, or let a drone run and count the japa.

    Nadi takes its inspiration from tantric teaching and Indian classical raga; it doesn't claim to represent either faithfully.
