    About Tratak

    The Sri Yantra is drawn as nine enclosures, the āvaraṇas, nested around the bindu. In the Journey the light moves through them one by one: from the square of gates, through the two lotuses and the rings of triangles, to the central point, and back out again.

    The enclosure you have reached glows; those you have passed stay faintly lit. Throughout, Shiva's four upward triangles and Shakti's five downward triangles keep two rhythms in conversation, drawing closer as you move inward until they meet at the central triangle. At the bindu everything rests.

    Dance sets every part moving at once, each in its own rhythm: the gates ripple, the circles and petals chase and shimmer, the two currents cross, and waves of light run through the rings of triangles. The figure changes every eight bars.

    Temple colours the yantra the way painted Sri Yantras often are, though painters and texts vary: the gates in white, yellow and blue; red outer petals and white inner petals; Shiva's upward triangles white and Shakti's downward triangles red; red rings around a white central triangle; and a red bindu. Cyber carries the same scheme into neon: white becomes ice and cyan, red becomes magenta, yellow becomes amber.

    At the end, the yantra holds still for a few seconds. Close your eyes and rest with the afterimage; after a minute a soft chime calls you back. Click or press any key to end the rest early.

    Pulse is the original rhythm: gates on the beat, triangles on eighth notes, lotus on seven over eight. Still keeps the yantra steady.
